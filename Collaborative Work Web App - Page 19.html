Indefinite & Definite Articles — Collaborative Work

Lesson A: Good Science, Bad Science (Page 19) — Articles (a, an, the, or X)

Rule 1: First vs. Second Mention

Use a or an when you mention a singular countable noun for the first time (or X for plural/uncountable). When you refer to the same item again, use the.

"With just one cell from a live or dead animal, an exact copy of the animal can be created."

Rule 2: General Statements (Countable)

If you use a plural noun to make a general statement, do not use an article (X). However, if you make the same statement using a singular noun, use the or a / an.

"...scans of activity patterns in X human brains." vs. "...in the / a human brain."

Rule 3: General Statements (Uncountable)

When making a general statement, do not use an article (X) with uncountable nouns (technology, education, shopping, love, DNA, storage, etc.).

"X DNA offers the longest duration for digital X storage."

Rule 4: Superlatives, Sequences & Time

Use the with superlatives and sequence markers (first, last, next), but do not use the (X) with time expressions (last night, next month, next year).

"The first attempts identified simple thoughts."
